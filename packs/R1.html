Week 1 recall day

Sunday 1 November. 2 hours, lighter day. Today you check what has stuck from the first week of packs (M1–M5, D1–D4, E1–E5). You are not learning new material today. You are finding out what to reread.

How to run today

Block Time What you do What you need
Part A 60 min Sit the Machines 2024 mid-sem test under exam conditions. Then mark it with the guide below. The test questions below (the same as “ees ug 24 test rv1”), calculator, your open-book notes. Do not open the solutions until the hour is up.
Break 5–10 min Stand up and get away from the desk.
Part B 45 min 24 mixed recall questions across all three subjects. About 1.5–2 min each. Answer each on paper before you open its <details>. Paper, calculator. Notes closed for the first pass.
Part C 15 min Fill in the gaps checklist using your Part A and Part B results. Pick the 3 packs you will reread next. This page.

Rules for today.

  • Part A is timed and open book, like the real test. Budget 1.5 min per mark (40 marks in 60 min). If a part takes more than twice its budget, skip it and come back.
  • Part B is closed book on the first pass. The point is recall. If you can’t answer, write “?” and the method you would try, then check. On the second pass, you can look things up for the ones you missed.
  • Mark yourself honestly. For each question, record ✓ (right, fast), ~ (right but slow, or needed a hint) or ✗ (wrong or blank). Those marks feed Part C.
  • Machines quantities are rms unless the question says peak.

Q8 Short-Answer Question [4]

2024 test Q8 (4 marks). 8) Short-Answer Question [ 4 marks ] Answer the following question using words only (that is, without using sketches or equations). Consider an ideal transformer driving a resistive load of resistance . Carefully explain what would be the effect on the output voltage and current if the load resistance was doubled. Carefully explain why and how this would be different for a real transformer.

Model answer and marking

The question says “words only”, so no equations or sketches.

Ideal transformer [2].

  • The output voltage does not change. It is set only by the input voltage and the turns ratio, and the load current does not affect it.
  • The output current halves. With the voltage fixed, doubling the load resistance halves the current (Ohm’s law). The input current also halves, because it is the output current scaled by the turns ratio.

Real transformer [2].

  • The output voltage rises slightly. A real transformer has series winding resistance and leakage reactance. When the load current falls, the voltage drop across these series elements falls, so more of the supply voltage reaches the output (the regulation improves).
  • The output current still falls, but to slightly more than half the original value, because the output voltage is now slightly higher.

Official points: the same four points. Each is worth 1 mark. You only get the mark for each “what” if you also give the “why”.
