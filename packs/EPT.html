EDC practice test: marking guide

How to use this: Sit the paper first (“Resources/Practice test_ENGE2003.pdf”): 90 minutes, timed, open book, calculator, no peeking at this guide. Then put your pen down and mark it question by question, opening each <details> block only when you are ready to compare. Be strict: award a mark only if your script shows the line that earns it. Write your score per question in the table under “After marking” and use that table to plan your revision.

The paper has 8 questions and 50 marks. The formula table is on page 2 of the paper (before Q1), not at the back. Every circuit value in this guide was read from the paper’s own figures and matches the values stated in your Test 1 Reference (p. 17–21). Every number was recomputed in Python.

What earns method marks on this paper.

  • Write the formula in symbols first, then substitute, then the answer with units. For example, , then numbers, then “= 0.179 V”. If the number is wrong but the formula and substitution are right, you still get most of the marks.
  • State the model you are using in words: “third PWL model: each diode = ideal diode + 0.6 V + 0.2 kΩ”, “active mode: V, ”, “ideal diode”.
  • “Redraw” parts (Q3(a), Q7(b)) are worth 2 and 4 marks. Draw them neatly and label every element value and every node you later use (, , , ).
  • “Verify” parts (Q3(b)) need a stated conclusion: “I = 15.3 µA > 0, so all three diodes conduct.” A number without the sentence loses the mark.
  • When the question tells you to assume a state (Q3, Q7, Q8), check it at the end in one line: current > 0, .

Before moving on from each question, check these:

  1. Units. Are kΩ and mA paired? Is µA written as µA, not mA?
  2. Sign and direction. Do the current arrows on your sketch match the signs you used?
  3. Is the state-assumption check written down?
  4. Is the final answer underlined or boxed, with units?

Question-by-question marking guide

Question 1: multiple choice [5 marks]

Paper wording. Circle the correct answer in the following multiple-choice questions (I to V).

I. When a PN junction is formed, why does a depletion region develop near the junction?
a) Electrons and holes recombine near the junction, leaving behind ionized dopants
b) The semiconductor becomes electrically insulating everywhere
c) All free electrons move from the N-region into the P-region permanently
d) The applied voltage removes all charge carriers from the semiconductor

II. The small-signal or dynamic resistance of a diode is related to:
a) The slope of the I–V characteristic at the operating point
b) The total length of the diode leads
c) The reverse breakdown voltage only
d) The DC supply resistance only

III. The main advantage of full-wave rectification over half-wave rectification is that full-wave rectification:
a) Uses only the negative half of the input waveform
b) Uses both half-cycles of the input waveform
c) Completely eliminates ripple without a capacitor
d) Requires no semiconductor devices

IV. Why is the base region of a BJT made relatively thin?
a) To allow most injected carriers to cross the base and reach the collector
b) To prevent carriers from reaching the collector
c) To increase the base resistance as much as possible
d) To eliminate the depletion region at the collector-base junction

V. Why are DC and AC analyses of a transistor amplifier often performed separately?
a) A transistor can operate with either DC or AC but never both
b) DC analysis establishes the operating point, while AC analysis examines small variations around that point
c) AC analysis determines the transistor’s doping concentration
d) DC analysis is only needed when the transistor is damaged

Model answer
Part Answer One-line reason
I (a) Carriers diffuse across and recombine near the junction, leaving immobile ionised donors (+, n side) and acceptors (−, p side). That bound charge is the depletion region.
II (a) , the inverse slope of the – curve at the Q-point.
III (b) It uses both half-cycles. That gives a higher DC output and twice the ripple frequency. It does not remove ripple without a capacitor, so (c) is wrong.
IV (a) Injected carriers cross the thin base before they recombine, so most reach the collector. That is why and is large.
V (b) DC analysis sets the Q-point. AC analysis linearises the device around that point (small-signal model).

This agrees with the Test 1 Reference.

Mark breakdown: 1 mark per correct letter. There are no part marks.

Most likely ways to lose marks:

  • Changing (I) to (c) because “electrons move to the p side” sounds right. Diffusion stops when the field balances it, and it is not “all” electrons, “permanently”.
  • Picking (c) in III because full-wave ripple is smaller. Smaller is not the same as eliminated.
